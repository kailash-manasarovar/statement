## Sullivan

- It's January and we've just buried my aunt Geraldine.
- Geraldine worked for Pan Am and had been called out at the last minute to work as the purser on Pan Am 103 which was bombed over Lockerbie.
- My mum invites everyone back to our house after the funeral and feeds them.
- There are *hundreds* of people in our house that day.
- I'm upstairs hiding from it all.
- Dad meets a man called Sullivan.
- Sullivan works/had worked for Pan Am in a management role of some sort and knew Geraldine well.
- Dad realizes he is the man Geraldine had told him about in the past; the man that brings the thick black briefcase onto the plane at Heathrow.
- Geraldine takes the bag and keeps it in the purser's office and the bag is picked up in New York by someone else.
- My dad remembers what his sister had told him about the thick black bag - it's probably common practice - as he's talking with this man Sullivan in our dining room.
- Or perhaps he remembered the details after meeting the man because the more significant thing for him was how the man had made him feel.
- Damien said he *felt* an intense negative energy emanating from the man Sullivan, in his mannerisms, the patterns of his voice, that wasn't explained by grief alone.
- It was unusual, he said; he may have said he felt the man was suffering from guilt (and my dad may well recognize that particular emotion in a person).
- My dad became firmly convinced that the man Sullivan had somehow been involved in the bombing. 
- The feeling he got from him, and then the memory of what Geraldine had told him regarding the bag, made him certain.
- Dad told this story again and again, to everyone, loudly.
- He still does if you ask him about it. He hasn't changed his story.
- Whenever dad tells anyone about this, he starts off with the energy-emanating part of the story before retelling what Geraldine had told him, as if this had been the bigger, more obvious thing in his mind.
- He told this story to so many people without realizing the Americans were listening very carefully to everything going on at 31, and still are.

### Hillsborough

- The plane was supposed to have gone down in the Atlantic like the Air India jet a few years before, disappearing any evidence.
- Instead, evidence fell on over 2000 square kilometers of the Scottish countryside, bodies littering people's gardens and hanging off their roofs.
- It was an absolute disaster and the Americans felt they needed to silence media interest.
- They hatched probably the most evil distraction plan anyone has ever come up with which was the Hillsborough disaster.
- Strangely I was watching the match that day.
- I don't like football very much so I'm not sure why I was watching it but the match was stopped by the referee and I saw those people dying in the stands.
- I'm told criminal gangs in Liverpool were not convinced this was an accident and were paid off my the Americans for their silence.
- I heard even the referee got a pay off.

### Silencing dad

- The Americans continued to be concerned about my dad's meeting with Sullivan and his inability to shut up about it, and so they hatched a silencing plan.
- I believe they organized the Tottenham rape gangs headed by Winston May and Nicky to target me that summer to make "sex tapes".
- It is my view that for the Tottenham rape gangs to have been able to gain access to me, the Americans would have required help from people close to me.
- I believe my schoolfriend Geetha, her brother Arul, and her mum Sonia Singham can shed light on how this was arranged.

The woman hated me so much, never spoke to me, but was very vocal about all-these-thing-that-had-happened-she-needed-me-to-know-about... didn't make any sense to me at the time.
